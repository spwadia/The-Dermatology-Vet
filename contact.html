Before you book

Where are you?

On the 5th floor of NECO Alpha, Ingawale Nagar, Pimple Nilakh, Pune — in the same premises as The Eye Vet. Pune is our only clinic for now. If you are further away, start with a tele-consult.

Do I need a referral from my own vet?

It helps a great deal, and we prefer it — your veterinarian’s history, previous test results and treatment record save time and money at the first consultation. But you can also book directly. Either way we write to your own vet and the case goes back to them.

Will you take my animal away from my regular vet?

No. We are a referral service only. We see the skin problem, send a written plan back to your veterinarian, and they continue to look after everything else. No referral commission changes hands in either direction.

How long does a first consultation take?

Between 45 and 60 minutes. Please allow longer on the day in case we need to take samples or run cytology while you wait.

What should I bring?

Any previous test results and clinical records, the packaging or photographs of every medication, shampoo and food your animal is currently on, and — if you can — photographs of the skin at its worst. Please do not bath the animal for 48 hours beforehand, and do not start any new treatment in the week before.

Will my pet be cured?

Sometimes. Parasitic, infectious and food-related disease can usually be resolved completely. Atopic dermatitis and several other conditions are controlled rather than cured — the goal is a comfortable animal on the least medication that achieves it. We will tell you honestly which category your animal is in at the first visit.

How much does it cost?

Fees are confirmed when you book, and we give a written estimate before any procedure or test. Dermatology is managed over months rather than in a single visit, so at the point of diagnosis we will also set out what a year of good control is likely to cost.

Do you see cats, horses, birds and exotic pets?

Yes — all of them. Equine cases are seen on the yard. Avian, reptile and small-mammal cases are seen jointly with exotics colleagues in the group where the species or the anaesthetic calls for it.
