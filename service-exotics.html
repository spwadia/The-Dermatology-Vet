Exotics & Avian

Feather-destructive disorders, scaly face in budgerigars, dysecdysis in reptiles and pododermatitis in rabbits and guinea pigs.

Skin and feather disease in exotic companion animals is rarely only skin deep — it is usually husbandry, nutrition or an underlying systemic disease showing on the outside.

Birds

  • Feather-destructive behaviour — worked up medically before it is called behavioural, in partnership with The Behaviour Vet.
  • Scaly face (Knemidocoptes) in budgerigars and passerines.
  • Pododermatitis — bumblefoot, where perch design is usually the real prescription.

Reptiles

  • Dysecdysis — retained shed, and the humidity and hydration failures behind it.
  • Scale rot and thermal burns from substrate and heat-source problems.
  • Abscesses and mite infestations.

Small mammals

  • Rabbits and guinea pigs — pododermatitis, Cheyletiella, Trixacarus, and the barbering that follows a dental or an arthritic problem.
  • Ferrets — symmetrical alopecia, which is adrenal disease until proven otherwise.

Exotic cases are seen jointly with The Exotics Vet where the species or the anaesthetic calls for it.
