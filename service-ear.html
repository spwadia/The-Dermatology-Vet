The Chronic Ear

Video-otoscopy, deep ear flushing under general anaesthesia, myringotomy and middle-ear sampling for the ear that keeps coming back.

A chronic ear is not an ear problem. It is a skin problem that happens to be in an ear, plus whatever the last six courses of ear drops have left behind.

Why ears relapse

Otitis externa runs at roughly 8–14% of dermatology caseload in Indian series. Almost every chronic case has three layers: a primary cause (allergy, a foreign body, a parasite, an endocrine disease), predisposing anatomy, and perpetuating factors — biofilm, resistant rods, a stenotic canal, a ruptured ear drum. Treating only the infection treats only the layer you can see.

Video-otoscopy

We examine the canal and the ear drum under general anaesthesia with a video-otoscope, on a screen you can see afterwards. That makes three things possible that a hand-held otoscope cannot:

  • A deep flush under direct vision — removing the debris and biofilm that topical treatment cannot penetrate.
  • Assessment of the ear drum — which decides whether a given medication is safe to use at all.
  • Myringotomy and middle-ear sampling — where otitis media is suspected, which is the most commonly missed reason an ear will not clear.

Foreign bodies, polyps and small masses can be retrieved or sampled in the same procedure.

When surgery is the kind answer. For an end-stage canal that has closed and calcified, total ear canal ablation is a cure rather than a failure. We will say so plainly, and arrange it with a surgeon in the group.
